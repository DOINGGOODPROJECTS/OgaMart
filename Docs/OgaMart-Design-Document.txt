OGAMART
1. Game Concept
OgaMart is a mobile game where players start with a small grocery shop and transform it into a successful supermarket empire while developing a lively neighborhood.
The game combines three experiences:
Supermarket Management: Stock products, serve customers, earn money, hire employees, and upgrade the store.
Neighborhood Development: Build coffee shops, bakeries, restaurants, cinemas, and other businesses that generate income and attract customers.
Living Neighborhood: Meet recurring characters, discover their stories, and make business decisions that influence customer happiness and neighborhood development.
Our competitive advantage: Unlike a conventional supermarket idle game, OgaMart connects business growth with the lives of neighborhood residents.
The game must remain easy to understand. Most routine actions should require only one or two taps.
Core gameplay loop:
STOCK → SELL → COLLECT → UPGRADE → UNLOCK → EXPAND → DISCOVER → REPEAT
The objective is to make the first minute enjoyable while gradually introducing strategic decisions, characters, and new businesses.

3. How Players Play — Freedom-Based Gameplay
3.1 Core Gameplay Philosophy
OgaMart will adopt a player-driven, open-ended gameplay system.
Players should have the freedom to manage their supermarket, interact with customers, develop businesses, customize their environment, and explore the neighborhood according to their individual preferences.
The game must not force players to follow a single predefined sequence of activities or restrict them to one management style.
Players can choose to operate their supermarket actively, automate business operations, concentrate on neighborhood expansion, interact with residents, or combine these activities.
Core Principle: Players decide how they want to play, manage, and grow their business.
Freedom applies to available gameplay systems, while purchases, construction, and upgrades remain subject to resources, progression requirements, and game rules.

3.3 Player-Controlled Supermarket Management
Players should have flexibility in how they operate their supermarkets.
They can:
Manually restock products or enable automatic restocking when available.
Allow employees to operate checkout counters or participate in optional checkout activities.
Choose which products to stock and sell.
Set product prices when pricing management is unlocked.
Select suppliers and manage deliveries.
Hire, train, assign, and upgrade employees.
Arrange eligible shelves, refrigerators, checkout counters, and decorations.
Expand supermarket departments in their preferred order when requirements are satisfied.
Prioritize profitability, customer satisfaction, or business expansion.
Upgrade equipment according to their preferred strategy.
The game should not require players to complete unnecessary repetitive tasks when automation is available.
3.4 Freedom to Explore and Interact
Players can explore accessible supermarket and neighborhood areas.
They can interact with customers, employees, suppliers, business owners, and recurring characters.
Interactions may include conversations, business negotiations, accepting missions, purchasing products, and responding to neighborhood requests.
Players should be able to explore without being forced to complete missions immediately.
Optional activities must remain optional unless they are necessary to introduce an essential gameplay mechanic.
3.5 Flexible Neighborhood Development
Players should be able to develop their neighborhoods according to their preferences.
They can:
Choose which available businesses to construct.
Select eligible building locations.
Upgrade existing businesses.
Arrange decorations and landscaping.
Develop commercial or residential areas where supported.
Choose which neighborhood opportunities to pursue.
Interact with residents and local business owners.
Participate in optional neighborhood events.
The neighborhood should reflect the player's decisions rather than follow an identical layout for every player.

3.7 Nonlinear Gameplay and Progression
OgaMart will use flexible progression.
Players should have several available objectives rather than being required to complete every activity in one fixed order.
For example, after unlocking the first neighborhood businesses, a player may choose to upgrade the supermarket, construct a coffee shop, complete a character mission, or improve customer satisfaction.
The player can return to other activities later.
Levels will introduce new opportunities, but they should not unnecessarily restrict previously unlocked gameplay.
Essential tutorials can introduce controls and mechanics, but should become skippable or unobtrusive once the player understands them.
3.8 Freedom With Meaningful Consequences
Players should be free to make different business decisions.
However, their choices must produce understandable consequences.
For example:
Higher prices may increase profit margins but reduce demand.
Lower prices may attract more customers but reduce profit per item.
Hiring employees increases operating costs but improves capacity.
Investing in neighborhood businesses creates new income opportunities.
Improving customer service increases satisfaction.
Neglecting inventory may result in missed sales.
The game should encourage experimentation rather than punish players excessively for making mistakes.
Players should be able to recover from poor business decisions through normal gameplay.

Player Learning and Tutorial Experience
Players should learn OgaMart's gameplay through short, interactive prompts, visual indicators, and direct actions rather than lengthy explanations or mandatory tutorials.
The game should follow a Learn by Playing approach, allowing players to discover features naturally while exploring and managing their supermarket and neighborhood.
Tutorial guidance should be:
Short and clear: Use simple instructions such as “Tap to Restock,” “Collect Earnings,” or “Explore Neighborhood.”
Optional: Allow players to skip, dismiss, or revisit tutorials.
Contextual: Display guidance only when players encounter unfamiliar features.
Interactive: Teach mechanics through actual gameplay rather than lengthy text.
Nonrestrictive: Never unnecessarily prevent players from exploring or performing other available actions.
Adaptive: Recognize actions players have already completed and avoid repeating instructions.
Visual: Use subtle highlights, animated arrows, and icons to guide players.
Flexible: Allow players to learn mechanics in their preferred order and at their own pace.
Core Design Principle: OgaMart should guide players without controlling their decisions. Players must feel free to explore, experiment, and develop their businesses while learning naturally through gameplay.
3.11 Game Design Requirements
The development team must ensure that:
Players can freely explore accessible game environments.
Multiple gameplay activities are available simultaneously.
Players are not forced into a single management style.
Manual and automated operations coexist where supported.
Eligible equipment and decorations can be repositioned.
Neighborhood layouts allow meaningful customization.
Missions and character interactions do not unnecessarily interrupt gameplay.
Players can change their business strategies.
The interface adapts to the selected object or activity.
The game remains simple enough for casual players while providing depth for those who want greater control.

5. Neighborhood Development
5.1 Neighborhood Development Philosophy
OgaMart will feature a dynamic, interactive, and customizable neighborhood where players can build, manage, expand, and develop businesses and community facilities according to their preferences.
The neighborhood should not simply function as a collection of buildings generating passive income.
Instead, it should become a living business ecosystem where supermarkets, local businesses, residents, employees, suppliers, and community facilities interact.
Players should have the freedom to determine how their neighborhoods develop, which businesses they establish, where eligible buildings are positioned, and how they allocate resources.
Core Principle: Players should have the freedom to create and develop their own unique neighborhoods rather than follow a predetermined construction sequence.
Neighborhood development will combine:
Business construction and management.
Neighborhood customization.
Resident interactions.
Local economic activity.
Community development.
Business investment and expansion.
Dynamic environmental changes.
The neighborhood will evolve based on player decisions, available resources, and business activities.

6. Living Neighborhood and Character Stories
6.1 Character Design Philosophy
OgaMart will feature a lively cast of distinctive, expressive, and memorable characters who bring personality, humor, drama, and emotional depth to the supermarket and neighborhood.
Rather than relying on generic customers with identical appearances and behaviors, OgaMart will introduce recognizable residents with unique physical features, personalities, occupations, preferences, relationships, and personal stories.
Characters should be visually memorable enough that players can recognize them immediately, even without seeing their names.
The character design will emphasize:
Exaggerated Physical Features: Distinctive body shapes, hairstyles, facial expressions, clothing, accessories, and silhouettes.
Strong Personalities: Funny, dramatic, demanding, generous, competitive, flirtatious, ambitious, or mysterious personalities.
Unique Animations: Each major character should have recognizable walking, shopping, talking, celebrating, and reacting animations.
Memorable Dialogue: Short, humorous, expressive conversations that reflect each character's personality.
Dynamic Relationships: Characters develop friendships, rivalries, business partnerships, and personal connections.
Player-Driven Interactions: Players choose whether and how to interact with characters.
Meaningful Consequences: Character interactions can influence customer loyalty, business opportunities, employment, and neighborhood development.
Cultural Personality: Characters can draw inspiration from the humor, fashion, expressions, and social interactions of lively African neighborhoods while remaining relatable to international audiences.
Core Principle: Every major character should have a distinctive appearance, memorable personality, recognizable behavior, and meaningful role in the game.
Characters should be entertaining without requiring players to complete their stories to progress through unrelated gameplay.
6.2 Character Appearance and Visual Identity
OgaMart will adopt a colorful, stylized cartoon character design.
Character proportions may be exaggerated to create humor and instantly recognizable silhouettes.
Characters should vary in:
Height and body proportions.
Hairstyles and hair colors.
Facial expressions.
Clothing styles.
Accessories.
Walking styles.
Voice and sound effects.
Personality and emotional reactions.
Some characters may have oversized glasses, dramatic hairstyles, large bellies, broad shoulders, exaggerated hips, distinctive facial features, or unusual fashion choices.
These features should make the characters memorable and contribute to their personalities.

7. Neighborhood Ripple System
7.1 Ripple System Philosophy
The Neighborhood Ripple System is one of OgaMart's signature gameplay features.
It connects player decisions, supermarket operations, neighborhood businesses, recurring characters, and community development into one interconnected simulation.
Unlike traditional supermarket tycoon games, where player decisions primarily influence revenue and upgrades, OgaMart will demonstrate how business decisions can influence the surrounding neighborhood.
Players should experience a world that responds to their actions.
For example:
Increasing supermarket prices may improve profit margins but reduce customer satisfaction.
Hiring neighborhood residents may improve business productivity and community relationships.
Constructing a bakery may attract customers, create employment opportunities, and increase neighborhood activity.
Supporting a local entrepreneur may unlock new products and business partnerships.
Opening a cinema may increase evening pedestrian traffic and benefit nearby businesses.
Neglecting inventory may cause customers to shop elsewhere.
Improving public spaces may attract additional residents and visitors.
Core Principle: Every meaningful player decision should have understandable consequences that can influence the supermarket, neighborhood, characters, or business ecosystem.
Not every minor action needs to trigger a Ripple event. The system should prioritize decisions that meaningfully affect gameplay.
Players must remain free to experiment with different business strategies without being forced to follow a particular development path.
